# Wayfarer Atlas

A free, self-owned fantasy map maker in the style of 16th–17th century engraved maps (ink on parchment, light hand-tinted washes). One self-contained HTML file, no build step, no dependencies beyond Google Fonts. Owner: Cameron (D&D DM). Desktop is the main target; phone is a rough-outlining mode only. A map is either a world/region/city map or a dungeon sheet (`S.kind`), in the manner of Dungeon Scrawl.

## Running it

Open `wayfarer-atlas.html` in a browser. That is the whole app. Maps are stored in the browser (IndexedDB, falling back to localStorage); "Save map file" writes JSON.

## Hard rules from the owner

- Engraved style only. No colour themes, no cartoonish art. Nothing with a "face" or comic-book shape. Serious, gritty linework.
- Light comes from the left: shading goes on the right of raised things, and on the LEFT inside hollows (craters, pits).
- Mirrored stamps must be redrawn mirrored with shading still on the right, never flipped as a picture.
- Colour washes stay faint and desaturated; ink marks stay sparse. Do not let it get busy.
- He values honest pushback and wants weak results flagged, not hidden.

## Architecture (all in the one file)

- State `S = {w,h,name,lands,cliffs,paints,lines,stamps,labels,grid,frame,rhumb,scale,layers,active}`. Lists are immutable (replace on edit) so undo snapshots share arrays. `snap()`, `pushUndo()`, `loadState()`, `migrate()`, `serialize()`.
- Render scale `R` (1 desktop, .5 phone, higher for export). Map-sized buffers are created in `initBuffers()`; draw in map units.
- `rebuildLand()` builds the land mask (rivers are carved out of it). `compose(full)` builds the base picture: paper, sea, coast lining, land, realms, cliffs. `drawVectors(c,k)` draws lines, stamps, labels per layer, then multiplies `tintC` (all colour washes) over everything.
- Terrain: ink marks are tiles from `INK_TILES` painted into `terrainC`; washes go to small blurred layers `washLand` / `washSea`; `buildTint()` combines them. `cleanInk()` removes marks cut by coasts, rivers and cliff edges.
- Stamps: `STAMP_SETS` → `STAMP_GROUPS` → `SKETCH_DRAW[kind](c,n)` drawn in a 40-unit box, ground at y≈14. Helpers: `engInk`, `engScratch`, `engShade`, `engPoly`, `engForm`, `engLimb`, `engHump`, `engTree`, `engHouse`, `engTower`, plan-view `cRoof`, `cSpire`, `cRound`. Use `EX(x)` for x positions so mirroring works (`ENG_M`). `paintStamp` scales pen weight (`penOf`) and hatch density (`ENG_K`) with stamp size. Sprites are cached in `sprites`.
- Drawing only what is seen: `draw()` passes `drawVectors` the part of the map in view (`vp`), so stamps and labels outside it are skipped; exports and thumbnails pass none and draw everything. Sprites (`sprite()`) are capped at 512 px (1024 when exporting), held to `SPRITE_PIXELS` by dropping the least recently used, and made under a per-frame time budget (`spriteBudgetMs`): a stamp whose sprite is not ready is drawn from another size already made and `spritesPending` asks for another frame to sharpen it. Zooming in once froze the page for seconds and used about a gigabyte; keep new per-stamp work inside this budget.
- Trees and ground cover are never drawn inside a mountain's body: `MTN_BODY` gives each mountain-like kind an ellipse (half-width, height, depth), `inMountain()` tests a point, `coverHidden()` caches which Trees/Plants stamps fall inside and `drawVectors` skips them; the plant generator and the plants brush use the same test. A new mountain-like kind needs an entry here as well as in `BASE_FADE`.
- `BASE_FADE` thins mountain-like sprites (`mountain`, `snowpeak`, `highpeak`, `hill`, `volcano`, `mesa`, `crag`) to transparent at their foot, so there is no hard line where a stamp meets the ground; the numbers are box-y values where the fade starts and ends, and a new mountain-like kind needs an entry.
- Hand-placed hatching in building stamps (`engHouse`, `engTower`, `cRoof`, cottage) divides its spacing by `ENG_K` so bigger stamps get more strokes; keep doing this for any new hatch loop. `engFurrow` lays ploughed rows across a field polygon.
- Fields: `fieldplot` (8 variants), `orchard` and `vineyard` (3 each) are listed in `VARIANTS`; the city generator mixes them in the outskirts.
- Roads: `drawRoads` / `roadNet` cut roads at crossings and at ends that land within 12 units of another road, fit the dashes per piece so every arm starts with a dash at the junction, and add a small blot. Drawing only; the saved lines are untouched.
- Dungeons (`S.kind==='dungeon'`, `S.dun={hatch,shadow}`): floors are ordinary `S.lands` entries flagged `floor:1` (`sharp:true` = straight-walled room that keeps its corners, see `sharpPoly`; `sharp+brush` = flat-ended corridor ribbon, see `sharpCorridor`; `mode:'sea'` digs a hole; `tex` = flag/plank/dirt/none). Inner walls are lines of kind `dwall`. `compose()` hands over to `composeDungeon(full)`: floor mask in `dgF`, a distance field over the rock (`distField`) drives the hatching (`dgHatch`, thick at the wall, thinning out), the wall is the floor silhouette nudged round in ink, textures go in `dgT` (`dgTexture`), then the grid (clipped to floors) and a band of vertical shading down the LEFT inside of each wall. `full` is false while dragging, which skips the wide hatching and textures. Tools `room`, `corridor`, `dwall` are tagged `m:'d'` in `TOOLS` (world-only tools `m:'w'`); `buildRail()` filters by map kind. Polygon rooms, corridors and walls are click-by-click drafts (`draft`, `draftClick`, `draftEnd`); `gsnap` snaps to the grid (a one-square corridor runs down the middle of its squares, see `corrOff`).
- Dungeon stamps: set `dungeon`, groups Doors / Stairs & pits / Furnishings / Hazards & features, art at the end of `SKETCH_DRAW` (`dBox`, `dRing`, `dPost`). `DUN_FOOT` gives each kind's footprint in grid squares (default size = footprint x cell). `dungSnap` puts doors on the middle of a square's edge and turns them to cross the passage (it looks at the floor either side), and everything else on half-squares. `generateDungeon()` makes example dungeons (rooms, spanning-tree corridors, doors, themed rooms with props, monsters by room theme, and room numbers).
- Placing stamps: the next stamp is decided ahead of the click (`pend`, `rollPending`, `pending()`: shape, mirrored or not, random seed). The ghost on the map, the preview in the sticky Placement bar at the top of the stamp panel (size, rotation, mirror, shape stepper; keys `[` `]` step the shape and `X` mirrors) and the stamp a click places all read it, so what the ghost shows is what lands. Any stamp can be mirrored (no group is excluded). A stamp's art must honour `ENG_M`/`EX()` so a mirror is a real redraw with shading on the right; the audit that found stamps ignoring it compared each kind's flipped render with its plain one, so re-run something like it after adding art. Text stamps (letters, numbers) mirror as a true reflection.
- Opacity is a property of each stamp (`s.op`, 0-100, absent = 100), drawn in `drawStamp`; nothing is drawn at a fixed opacity (`STAMP_ALPHA` is empty). Set it in the Placement bar (`opts.stampOpacity`, also on the ghost) and on a selected stamp (`selOp`).
- Scatter (`scatterDab`): each step of a drag tries a few spots within `opts.scatter` (Spread) and keeps one only if it is at least `scatterGap` x the stamp size from every same-kind stamp already there, so stamps lie evenly; `scatterDensity` (Amount) sets the tries per step, `sizeVar` the size variation.
- Legacy plant stamps: `wildflowers` (8), `wildgrass` (4), `shrubs` (5), `thicket` (4), `saplings` (3), `ferns` (3) are no longer offered in the Stamp palette or made by the generator (flowers and grasses are ground paint, shrubs and saplings are the woodland edge, see below), but their art stays in `SKETCH_DRAW` so maps saved with them still draw, move and delete as before. It is built from `plLeaf`, `plPetal`, `HEAD[...]`, `plLump` and `plSapling`; keep the light from the left (hatching on the right half) if it is ever touched. The Plants group now holds only `bush`, `reeds`, `cactus`, `mushroom`, `stump`.
- Monsters (groups Humanoids / Beasts & vermin / Horrors) are seen from above, facing up the page; the stamp's rotation turns them. Build them from `mMan` (humanoids: shoulders, head, arms round a weapon, `mWpn`), `engForm`/`engLimb` bodies and `mHead`; never give one a face. Single characters are stamps `ch_0`..`ch_9`, `ch_A`..`ch_Z` (groups Numbers / Letters, `noflip`, variant 1 is ringed); room numbers are one sticker per digit, drawn with text so `sprites` is cleared when the fonts load. Dungeon kind names must not collide with existing `SKETCH_DRAW` names (a later `Object.assign` silently replaces the older art): the `basin`, `wellshaft`, `firepit`, `cratestack`, `giantspider` and `direwolf` names exist for that reason.
- A raised cliff (`mode:'up'`, h>0) is land: `rebuildLand()` adds it to the land mask, so a cliff that reaches past the coast extends the landmass (coast lining follows the plateau; the face and shadow hang over the water, unclipped). Pits and lowered areas do not. Because cliffs shape the coast, `invalidate()` rebuilds the land whenever `S.cliffs` changes identity (`cliffLandFor`).
- Cliffs: `rebuildCliffs()` (plateaus h>0, pits h<0, taper, facets, cast shadows). A taper's height runs from H x (1 - taper) at the low end up to H at the high end; `fadeLow(ctx,w,h,f0,f1)` fades a layer out along the slope as the height (a fraction f of full) falls from f1 to f0. The plateau's outline is drawn on its own layer (`O`/`ox`, via `od` in `inked`) so the edge can go first (f .2-.27) while the hatched face and shadow end soon after (f .14-.22), both over a very short stretch: the face shrinks geometrically to nothing, so only a sliver is left for the opacity fade, and a long soft fade looked rough: a real ramp has no crisp edge, and fading everything together once left half-visible double lines. Keep the outline layer cleared of its interior (`destination-out` A and B) or the ink fill covers the hatching.
- Lines: `LINES` kinds river, road, border, realm, street, wall, fence. Streets are drawn in two passes so junctions merge (`drawStreets`).
- Generators: `generateMap(W,H,kind,G)` (island/continent/archipelago) and `generateCity(W,H,G)`; `housesAlong()` lines buildings along streets.
- `generateMap` terrain is built from tectonic plates, all drawn from `mulberry(seed+1001)` and the seeded noise (never `rnd`), so a seed always repeats and nothing made after it is reshuffled: a Voronoi partition into 6-14 plates whose edges are bent at three scales (`bend`, so boundaries and the coasts that follow them are ragged), each plate drifting and continental or oceanic (which ones: `bias`/`nC` by kind); plate height blended across the join (`BLEND`); collisions uplift (`uplift`: mountains on continental crust, a trench `subs` on the oceanic side, an island arc between two oceanic plates), divergence digs a rift, sliding only roughens (`splat`/`side`); plus noise; then sea level is a quantile so the land is the share the kind and the Land slider ask for (about .3 island, .4 continent, .25 archipelago). Sea level is nudged so no cell is exactly 0 (a corner on the contour has no side and breaks the shoreline tracer), and rings are classed land or lake by signed area. `ridge` (what the mountain placement reads) is the noise ridge scaled down plus the uplift, so ranges follow the collisions.
- Lakes and rivers in `generateMap`: a priority-flood from the border (`filled`) finds basins; basins of five or more cells are dropped below sea level (deeper where the basin is deeper, outlines rounded by `smooth`) and traced by the same marching squares as `mode:'sea'`. Enclosed water of 150 cells or more is filled as a plain. Rivers: `down` is each cell's steepest lower neighbour on `filled`, `acc` is flow added up from the heights down, rivers are traced from the highest, most mountainous ground, a tributary stops at a river already drawn, a river reaching a lake ends at its shore (the line is cut wherever a point falls inside a lake outline) and begins again where the lake drains; width goes with sqrt(flow) at 60% of the way down. `riverCell` marks them for the town and hill placement.
- Heightmap: a generated map keeps `S.height` (`packHeight`: nx, ny, cs, heights in hundredths as base64 Int8, and a mask: 0 land, 1 sea, 2 lake); `S.hm` is the view flag (not undone: `restore` keeps it). `compose` hands over to `composeHeight` when it is on (shaded grey relief lit from the left, sea level mid-grey, lakes blue, rivers drawn over; `drawVectors` then skips stamps and the colour wash). A map with no stored heights gets `heightGrid()`'s estimate from its coast, cliffs and mountains.

- Ground in `generateMap`: `biome()` gives the base ground; `groundAt()` splits grass, meadow and steppe into drifts by three noise fields (`f` flowers, `g` tall grass, `v` which one) and returns a flower field (`FLOWER_GROUNDS`), a tall grass (`GRASS_GROUNDS`) or plain meadow, and the row strokes that become `S.paints` use it. No plant stamps are generated. The wood's edge is not decided here: it appears wherever open ground meets forest (`rebuildEco`).
- Mountains in `generateMap`: ridge cells (which follow the plate collisions) are joined into lines and only the longest few become ranges (`range`, count by map kind and `G.mountains`), peaks are spaced and thinned, small mountains are capped (~34 x `G.mountains`), then `highpeak` stamps (4 variants, size 150-200, `KIND_SIZE`) replace the small ones at the thickest part of a range, but only where the whole broad foot stands on land and no river or road runs through it (the art reaches almost a full size from its middle), so a map gets about 0-2 of them. The range name goes with the biggest knot of peaks, not the mean of all of them. A high peak is wider than the usual sprite canvas: `SPR_SIDE`/`sprSide(kind)` gives it 2.1 sizes (others 1.7) in `sprite`, `drawStamp` and `stampHit`; give any new oversized art an entry there. `engHump` takes `lw` (outline weight) and `shade` (light strokes on the shaded side of the snow); `highpeak` uses both. `SOLO_KINDS` are placed one at a time even in a scattering group. Default maps carry roughly a quarter of the small mountains they once did.
- Labels: `drawLabel` lays a soft-edged paper halo under the lettering (pass 0, `shadowBlur` scaled by the current transform) so a name reads over hatching, streets, rivers and mountains. Names are not moved out of the way; keep using the halo rather than placement logic.
- Dungeon room numbers (`generateDungeon`) go to the nearest spot not covered by a stair, door, prop or foe (the usual top-left spot first), so the numbering never appears to skip a room.
- Stamp names are global: the city's palisaded fort is `stockade` (Outskirts); `outpost` (Settlements) is the world's hill fort. `bones`, `web` and `pool` exist only in the dungeon art block. Check for duplicate method names across the `SKETCH_DRAW` blocks when adding art (the later one silently wins).
- Stamp palette: a search box (`opts.stampQuery`, list rebuilt by `stampListHTML()` on its own so the box keeps focus; with a query it lists every matching kind in every set, matched on kind or group name) and favourites (`favs`, kept in the library store under `'__favs'`, not in map files; stars via `stampGrid(..., stars)`, `toggleFav`, number keys 1-9). `chooseStamp(kind)` is the one place that sets the stamp, its size (dungeon footprint, `KIND_SIZE`) and set.
- Selection: `locked:true` on any object skips it in `hitTest`/`boxSelect`/reshape/line rubbing/nudge/delete and hides the transform handles; Lock/Unlock in the panel, Unlock all when nothing is selected, Alt-click picks a locked object (`hitTest(p,true)`); a padlock marks locked things under Select/Reshape. Shift-click and Shift-drag add to the selection (`makeSel`). `objBox(item)` is one thing's box (`selBox` is their union); `alignSel(mode)` aligns L/C/R/T/M/B and spreads H/V by centres, using `moveItems` once per item (a moved landmass still takes what is on it). A landmass moved by hand still carries locked things standing on it.
- Labels: effects live in `l.effects` as overrides only (defaults `LABEL_FX`: outline on, shadow and plate off), so old maps need no migration; `labelFx(l)` gives the effective set. `drawLabel` order: plate, outline halo, shadow, lettering. Choosing a style applies `LABEL_STYLES[..].fx` (Title: plate without halo, Note: shadow).
- History: `undoStack`/`redoStack` hold `{state,label}`; `pushUndo(label)` defaults the label from tool and selection (`undoLabel`), call sites that know better pass one. `goTo(i)` jumps to any step and redraws once; the History section (`histHTML`) is refreshed from `syncButtons()`. Cap is 60.
- Export: `maxScale()` allows `S.w*S.h*k*k*BUFFER_COUNT <= EXPORT_BUDGET` (15 full-size equivalents, 260e6). `exportPNG` checks the canvases it got (`buffersOK`) and shrinks every buffer but the picture to 1x1 (`releaseBuffers`, not null: the screen may still redraw) before encoding.
- `cleanInk()` redoes only the box that changed when the lands, cliffs and land are as they were and the paint list differs by added or removed strokes (undo included): it reads the box plus a 24 unit margin so marks are judged whole, and writes back only the box.
- Tints: `TINTS` lists the grounds each stamp kind may take; `sprite()` multiplies the colour in at `TINT_STRENGTH` (`tintSprite`), so ink stays black. A stamp with no `tint` stays plain; new placements default to Plain. Stamp art is called as `(c, n)`; the old fourth 'grown' argument is gone.
- Rotation snap (`opts.rotSnap`, Map settings): the rotation sliders (`ROT_IDS`) round to 15; the turn handle snaps when it is on or Shift is held. Dungeon snap toggles are one shared block for room, corridor, wall, floor and stamp tools.
- Shore: `rebuildCoast` subtracts visible river paths from the shore band and inner shading, so a river has no lining inside it.
- Library: `store`, `lib`, `saveNow()`, `openMap()`, `addMap()`.
- Tools are listed in `TOOLS` (`m:'w'` world only, `'d'` dungeon only, `'x'` not on the rail); the side panel is built by `renderPanel()`. Several tools do more than one job, and `etool()` says which job is being done: Terrain has modes Ground / Water / Cliffs (`opts.paintLayer`, the C key jumps to Cliffs), Wall also draws hedges, fences, field walls and palisades (`opts.wallKind`), Street also draws country roads (`opts.roadMode`, the old `road` line kind). The Border tool was removed; existing `border` lines still draw and can be reshaped. Pointer handling and the panel both dispatch on `etool()`, not `opts.tool`. Detail, Grid, Sheet and Show-by-kind sit in a closed `<details id="mapSettings">` at the bottom of the panel (`opts.settingsOpen`); Open lives in the Maps dialog.
- Ground types (`TERRAIN`, painted with the Terrain tool > Ground): groups Grass (`meadow` = plain, `grass`, `steppe`, `forest`, `marsh`), Grasses (`oats`, `feather`, `pampas`, `tussock`, named after the old wildgrass art) and Flowers (`daisies`, `poppies`, `lupins`, `bluebells`, `parsley`, `thistles`, `tulips`, `wildmix`, after the old flower heads), then Stone, Sand, Cold. Each is an `INK_TILES` recipe: a faint wash plus small sparse ink marks, tiled into `terrainC`; keep each mark small and stemless enough not to read as a mushroom. The ground marks are cut whole at coasts, banks and cliff edges by `cleanInk()`.
- The woodland edge (`rebuildEco`, layer `ecoC`, drawn just over the terrain in `compose`): not stamps, not saved, worked out from the paint every time the paints, lands, cliffs or land version change. The top ground at each low-res cell (`ECO_CELL`) is replayed from `S.paints` into a forest mask and an open-ground mask (`isOpenGround`: meadow, grass, steppe and the Grasses and Flowers groups); a distance field from the forest gives a band `ECO_REACH` wide on the open side; shrubs (`ecoShrub`) and saplings (`ecoSapling`, broadleaf or conifer), denser and more often saplings near the trees, are placed on a hashed lattice (`ECO_LATTICE`, `ecoHash`, so editing one place never reshuffles the rest). A mark is placed only if its whole bounding box plus a margin is on land (`landMask`) clear of cliff faces (`cliffSolidC`) and river banks, so no edge ever cuts one. A new ground that should grow scrub at its edge only needs adding to `isOpenGround`. Cliffs drawn from Terrain take `opts.cliffTaper` and `opts.cliffDir`.
- Dungeon floors can be painted: `S.fpaint` is a list of strokes `{tex,size,seed,pts}` laid over the rooms' own textures by `dgPaint()` in `composeDungeon` (cut to the floor; `erase` strokes clear what was there). The Floor tool has Paint / Fill a room (sets `land.tex`) / Rub out.

## Working conventions

- Keep it a single file. Make targeted edits; many lines are long, so match exact strings carefully.
- After any change, load the page in a headless browser and check for page errors, then exercise the affected tool. A good smoke test: new blank map → draw land, sea, cliff, paint, river, road, stamp, label → move a landmass → undo/redo → `serialize()` round trip → export at 2×.
- Look at rendered output before calling art finished; judge it at whole-map zoom and close up.

## Known gaps

- Reported but not yet fixed: roads are ruler-straight over long runs, city stamps overlap (cathedral, warehouses on the wall, a field across a road), the city river mouth has a pale fan and streets crossing the river show no bridges, the city shore is a straight line, dungeon corridor flagstones run as a band across the room they enter and the grid doubles the joints, an example map has no border/compass/cartouche, the cartouche is polygonal and over-hatched at large sizes, stairs read as brick, furniture shading is a hard grey block, realm fringe is heavy, paddock animals and hedges look crude, depth paints (shallow/open/deep/abyss) are barely different and ice floes look like bubbles, lakes fill with lining and rhumb lines cross lakes, rivers end in a blunt stub at the source and a tributary's banks cross the main river, tapered plateaus leave a sliver of face at the low end, a street across a town wall makes no gate and hand-drawn walls wobble, the gelatinous cube is a plain box and the goblin and beholder are still toy-like.

- Battle maps are undecided. Dungeons have no lighting or per-room colour, no stairs linking levels, and no way to rotate a room; round and cave rooms get no doors in the generator.
- A full dungeon compose is ~0.5 s on a software-rendered browser (hatching and textures dominate); dragging uses a cheaper compose.
- Dungeon art still to push: the humanoids share one body (`mMan`) and differ by head (`HEADS`), weapon and mantle; `zombie` and `ogre` are the plainest.
- City district names are not placed to avoid streets.
- Brush hardness no longer affects washes (they are always soft).
