# Wayfarer Atlas

A free, self-owned fantasy map maker in the style of 16th–17th century engraved maps (ink on parchment, light hand-tinted washes). One self-contained HTML file, no build step, no dependencies beyond Google Fonts. Owner: Cameron (D&D DM). Desktop is the main target; phone is a rough-outlining mode only. A map is either a world/region/city map or a dungeon sheet (`S.kind`), in the manner of Dungeon Scrawl.

## Running it

Open `wayfarer-atlas.html` in a browser. That is the whole app. Maps are stored in the browser (IndexedDB, falling back to localStorage); "Save map file" writes JSON.

## Hard rules from the owner

- Engraved style only. No colour themes, no cartoonish art. Nothing with a "face" or comic-book shape. Serious, gritty linework.
- Light comes from the left: shading goes on the right of raised things, and on the LEFT inside hollows (craters, pits).
- Mirrored stamps must be redrawn mirrored with shading still on the right, never flipped as a picture.
- Colour washes stay faint and desaturated; ink marks stay sparse. Do not let it get busy.
- He values honest pushback and wants weak results flagged, not hidden.

## Architecture (all in the one file)

- State `S = {w,h,name,lands,cliffs,paints,lines,stamps,labels,grid,frame,rhumb,scale,layers,active}`. Lists are immutable (replace on edit) so undo snapshots share arrays. `snap()`, `pushUndo()`, `loadState()`, `migrate()`, `serialize()`.
- Render scale `R` (1 desktop, .5 phone, higher for export). Map-sized buffers are created in `initBuffers()`; draw in map units.
- `rebuildLand()` builds the land mask (rivers are carved out of it). `compose(full)` builds the base picture: paper, sea, coast lining, land, realms, cliffs. `drawVectors(c,k)` draws lines, stamps, labels per layer, then multiplies `tintC` (all colour washes) over everything.
- Terrain: ink marks are tiles from `INK_TILES` painted into `terrainC`; washes go to small blurred layers `washLand` / `washSea`; `buildTint()` combines them. `cleanInk()` removes marks cut by coasts, rivers and cliff edges.
- Stamps: `STAMP_SETS` → `STAMP_GROUPS` → `SKETCH_DRAW[kind](c,n)` drawn in a 40-unit box, ground at y≈14. Helpers: `engInk`, `engScratch`, `engShade`, `engPoly`, `engForm`, `engLimb`, `engHump`, `engTree`, `engHouse`, `engTower`, plan-view `cRoof`, `cSpire`, `cRound`. Use `EX(x)` for x positions so mirroring works (`ENG_M`). `paintStamp` scales pen weight (`penOf`) and hatch density (`ENG_K`) with stamp size. Sprites are cached in `sprites`.
- Drawing only what is seen: `draw()` passes `drawVectors` the part of the map in view (`vp`), so stamps and labels outside it are skipped; exports and thumbnails pass none and draw everything. Sprites (`sprite()`) are capped at 512 px (1024 when exporting), held to `SPRITE_PIXELS` by dropping the least recently used, and made under a per-frame time budget (`spriteBudgetMs`): a stamp whose sprite is not ready is drawn from another size already made and `spritesPending` asks for another frame to sharpen it. Zooming in once froze the page for seconds and used about a gigabyte; keep new per-stamp work inside this budget.
- Trees and ground cover are never drawn inside a mountain's body: `MTN_BODY` gives each mountain-like kind an ellipse (half-width, height, depth), `inMountain()` tests a point, `coverHidden()` caches which Trees/Plants stamps fall inside and `drawVectors` skips them; the plant generator and the plants brush use the same test. A new mountain-like kind needs an entry here as well as in `BASE_FADE`.
- `BASE_FADE` thins mountain-like sprites (`mountain`, `snowpeak`, `highpeak`, `hill`, `volcano`, `mesa`, `crag`) to transparent at their foot, so there is no hard line where a stamp meets the ground; the numbers are box-y values where the fade starts and ends, and a new mountain-like kind needs an entry.
- Hand-placed hatching in building stamps (`engHouse`, `engTower`, `cRoof`, cottage) divides its spacing by `ENG_K` so bigger stamps get more strokes; keep doing this for any new hatch loop. `engFurrow` lays ploughed rows across a field polygon.
- Fields: `fieldplot` (8 variants), `orchard` and `vineyard` (3 each) are listed in `VARIANTS`; the city generator mixes them in the outskirts.
- Roads: `drawRoads` / `roadNet` cut roads at crossings and at ends that land within 12 units of another road, fit the dashes per piece so every arm starts with a dash at the junction, and add a small blot. Drawing only; the saved lines are untouched.
- Dungeons (`S.kind==='dungeon'`, `S.dun={hatch,shadow}`): floors are ordinary `S.lands` entries flagged `floor:1` (`sharp:true` = straight-walled room that keeps its corners, see `sharpPoly`; `sharp+brush` = flat-ended corridor ribbon, see `sharpCorridor`; `mode:'sea'` digs a hole; `tex` = flag/plank/dirt/none). Inner walls are lines of kind `dwall`. `compose()` hands over to `composeDungeon(full)`: floor mask in `dgF`, a distance field over the rock (`distField`) drives the hatching (`dgHatch`, thick at the wall, thinning out), the wall is the floor silhouette nudged round in ink, textures go in `dgT` (`dgTexture`), then the grid (clipped to floors) and a band of vertical shading down the LEFT inside of each wall. `full` is false while dragging, which skips the wide hatching and textures. Tools `room`, `corridor`, `dwall` are tagged `m:'d'` in `TOOLS` (world-only tools `m:'w'`); `buildRail()` filters by map kind. Polygon rooms, corridors and walls are click-by-click drafts (`draft`, `draftClick`, `draftEnd`); `gsnap` snaps to the grid (a one-square corridor runs down the middle of its squares, see `corrOff`).
- Dungeon stamps: set `dungeon`, groups Doors / Stairs & pits / Furnishings / Hazards & features, art at the end of `SKETCH_DRAW` (`dBox`, `dRing`, `dPost`). `DUN_FOOT` gives each kind's footprint in grid squares (default size = footprint x cell). `dungSnap` puts doors on the middle of a square's edge and turns them to cross the passage (it looks at the floor either side), and everything else on half-squares. `generateDungeon()` makes example dungeons (rooms, spanning-tree corridors, doors, themed rooms with props, monsters by room theme, and room numbers).
- Placing stamps: the next stamp is decided ahead of the click (`pend`, `rollPending`, `pending()`: shape, mirrored or not, random seed). The ghost on the map, the preview in the sticky Placement bar at the top of the stamp panel (size, rotation, mirror, shape stepper; keys `[` `]` step the shape and `X` mirrors) and the stamp a click places all read it, so what the ghost shows is what lands. Any stamp can be mirrored (no group is excluded). A stamp's art must honour `ENG_M`/`EX()` so a mirror is a real redraw with shading on the right; the audit that found stamps ignoring it compared each kind's flipped render with its plain one, so re-run something like it after adding art. Text stamps (letters, numbers) mirror as a true reflection.
- Monsters (groups Humanoids / Beasts & vermin / Horrors) are seen from above, facing up the page; the stamp's rotation turns them. Build them from `mMan` (humanoids: shoulders, head, arms round a weapon, `mWpn`), `engForm`/`engLimb` bodies and `mHead`; never give one a face. Single characters are stamps `ch_0`..`ch_9`, `ch_A`..`ch_Z` (groups Numbers / Letters, `noflip`, variant 1 is ringed); room numbers are one sticker per digit, drawn with text so `sprites` is cleared when the fonts load. Dungeon kind names must not collide with existing `SKETCH_DRAW` names (a later `Object.assign` silently replaces the older art): the `basin`, `wellshaft`, `firepit`, `cratestack`, `giantspider` and `direwolf` names exist for that reason.
- Cliffs: `rebuildCliffs()` (plateaus h>0, pits h<0, taper, facets, cast shadows). A taper's height runs from H x (1 - taper) at the low end up to H at the high end; `fadeLow(ctx,w,h,f0,f1)` fades a layer out along the slope as the height (a fraction f of full) falls from f1 to f0. The plateau's outline is drawn on its own layer (`O`/`ox`, via `od` in `inked`) so the edge can go first (f .2-.27) while the hatched face and shadow end soon after (f .14-.22), both over a very short stretch: the face shrinks geometrically to nothing, so only a sliver is left for the opacity fade, and a long soft fade looked rough: a real ramp has no crisp edge, and fading everything together once left half-visible double lines. Keep the outline layer cleared of its interior (`destination-out` A and B) or the ink fill covers the hatching.
- Lines: `LINES` kinds river, road, border, realm, street, wall, fence. Streets are drawn in two passes so junctions merge (`drawStreets`).
- Generators: `generateMap(W,H,kind,G)` (island/continent/archipelago) and `generateCity(W,H,G)`; `housesAlong()` lines buildings along streets.
- Open ground in `generateMap` (block after the trees): three large noise fields (`f` wildflowers, `g` wild grass, `v` which kind of flower) split grass, meadow and steppe into drifts, and a distance-to-forest field `fd` grades the wood's edge (1 cell: thicket/saplings/ferns; 2-4: shrubs/saplings; 5: sparse shrubs). The plants grow in clumps, not an even carpet: `STEP`-spaced clump centres inside a drift each get a radius, a count and (mostly) one kind, dense at the middle and thinning to a fringe, with bare ground between clumps (a second noise field, `.011`, leaves gaps); flower clumps have wild grass about their feet, and the wood's edge gets thicket/sapling/fern clumps at the trees and shrub groups beyond. A clump only grows where the drift it belongs to (`cls`) does; drifts under 18 cells are dropped. Each plant is drawn at a small fraction (`SIZE`, .26-.34) of its listed size, and in a lighter ink (`STAMP_ALPHA`, applied in `drawStamp` for map drawing but not for thumbnails). They were once drawn much larger and evenly spread, which looked wrong. Spacing uses its own stamp index (`occ`, `free`), because the generator's `clear` only knows about towns. Stamps: `wildflowers` (8 variants, `WF` heads), `wildgrass` (4), `shrubs` (5), `thicket` (4), `saplings` (3), `ferns` (3), all in the Plants group; the generator picks variants from the `v` field so a drift is mostly one kind of flower with some others mixed in.
- Mountains in `generateMap`: ridge cells are joined into lines and only the longest few become ranges (`range`, count by map kind and `G.mountains`), peaks are spaced and thinned, small mountains are capped (~34 x `G.mountains`), then 2-4 `highpeak` stamps (4 variants, size 150-200, `KIND_SIZE`) replace the small ones at the thickest part of a range. `SOLO_KINDS` are placed one at a time even in a scattering group. Default maps carry roughly a quarter of the small mountains they once did.
- Library: `store`, `lib`, `saveNow()`, `openMap()`, `addMap()`.
- Tools are listed in `TOOLS` (`m:'w'` world only, `'d'` dungeon only, `'x'` not on the rail); the side panel is built by `renderPanel()`. Several tools do more than one job, and `etool()` says which job is being done: Terrain has modes Ground / Water / Cliffs / Plants (`opts.paintLayer`, the C key jumps to Cliffs), Wall also draws hedges, fences, field walls and palisades (`opts.wallKind`), Street also draws country roads (`opts.roadMode`, the old `road` line kind). The Border tool was removed; existing `border` lines still draw and can be reshaped. Pointer handling and the panel both dispatch on `etool()`, not `opts.tool`. Detail, Grid, Sheet and Show-by-kind sit in a closed `<details id="mapSettings">` at the bottom of the panel (`opts.settingsOpen`); Open lives in the Maps dialog.
- Plants brush (`plantDab`, Terrain > Plants): scatters clumps of `PLANT_MIX` kinds at the generator's sizes (`PLANT_BASE` x `PLANT_FACTOR`), on land, clear of existing stamps; right button or Rub out deletes plants within the brush. Cliffs drawn from Terrain take `opts.cliffTaper` and `opts.cliffDir`.
- Dungeon floors can be painted: `S.fpaint` is a list of strokes `{tex,size,seed,pts}` laid over the rooms' own textures by `dgPaint()` in `composeDungeon` (cut to the floor; `erase` strokes clear what was there). The Floor tool has Paint / Fill a room (sets `land.tex`) / Rub out.

## Working conventions

- Keep it a single file. Make targeted edits; many lines are long, so match exact strings carefully.
- After any change, load the page in a headless browser and check for page errors, then exercise the affected tool. A good smoke test: new blank map → draw land, sea, cliff, paint, river, road, stamp, label → move a landmass → undo/redo → `serialize()` round trip → export at 2×.
- Look at rendered output before calling art finished; judge it at whole-map zoom and close up.

## Known gaps

- Battle maps are undecided. Dungeons have no lighting or per-room colour, no stairs linking levels, and no way to rotate a room; round and cave rooms get no doors in the generator.
- A full dungeon compose is ~0.5 s on a software-rendered browser (hatching and textures dominate); dragging uses a cheaper compose.
- Dungeon art still to push: the humanoids share one body (`mMan`) and differ by head (`HEADS`), weapon and mantle; `zombie` and `ogre` are the plainest.
- City district names are not placed to avoid streets.
- Brush hardness no longer affects washes (they are always soft).
